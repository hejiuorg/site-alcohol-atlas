On Monday 16 November 2026, the Paulée de Meursault will be held at Château de Meursault. It is the last act of Burgundy's Trois Glorieuses: the Confrérie des Chevaliers du Tastevin holds its chapter at Clos de Vougeot on Saturday, the Hospices de Beaune wine auction takes place under the Halles de Beaune on Sunday, and on Monday the village of Meursault closes the weekend with a lunch at which guests bring their own bottles. What the event is

Paulée literally means the meal after the harvest. In Burgundy it was the custom for growers and vineyard workers to eat together when the picking ended; the best-known surviving version is in Meursault. The 2026 event is not an auction but a lunch: guests sit at long tables, bring bottles to share with their neighbours, and the organisers award the Paulée literary prize during the meal; local tourism material puts the room at around 700 guests. Confusing “auction” with “dinner” is the most common misunderstanding of this event.

Another event is easy to confuse with it. Château de Meursault itself held the 22nd “Paulée du Château de Meursault” on 17 October 2026, a dinner for the estate's guests with a guest chef designing the menu, different in scale and access from the November Paulée de Meursault. This event is about the 16 November event that belongs to the Trois Glorieuses.

Dates and venue in 2026

  • Date: Monday 16 November 2026.
  • Start: around 12:00, with the lunch running into the afternoon.
  • Venue: Château de Meursault, Rue Charles Giraud, 21190 Meursault. According to local wine reporting, the 2026 edition is held in the estate's renovated vat room (cuverie); the entrance and meeting point are subject to the organiser's instructions.
  • Time zone: Europe/Paris (UTC+1 in winter).
  • The start here is 12:00 and the end is 23:59:59 on the same day, used only to mark the date. The organiser has not published an ending time, so do not read 23:59 as the moment the room empties.

Placing the lunch in the Trois Glorieuses timetable makes the date clear. On Sunday 15 November 2026, the 166th Hospices de Beaune wine auction takes place under the Halles de Beaune. On Monday 16 November at midday, the Paulée opens at Château de Meursault. The two towns are about eight kilometres apart, roughly fifteen minutes by car. In 2025 the Paulée was held outside the walls because of venue constraints; in 2026 it returns to the estate's vat room, a concrete change for this edition.

History and mechanics

The modern Paulée is usually traced to 1923. The grower Jules Lafon decided to revive the medieval harvest feast at which Cistercian monks invited their vineyard workers. He held the first Paulée in the vat room of his Meursault estate and invited 35 friends, each bringing wine and sharing a meal. Neighbouring growers joined, the event grew from a private banquet into a village gathering, and it eventually became fixed as the Monday lunch of the Trois Glorieuses. That origin explains the two core rules of the Paulée: guests bring bottles, and there is no fixed wine list.

Local wine reporting calls the 2026 edition the 93rd. That figure comes from Portail Vins; the organiser's page does not confirm it word for word, so this event treats it as a reference rather than a hard fact. What is clear is that the Paulée was revived with the Trois Glorieuses after the Second World War and became one of the most important social occasions in the Burgundy wine year. It is organised by the Paulée de Meursault team, hosted by Château de Meursault, and its seats are not sold through a single public platform.

The mechanics are the opposite of a normal wine dinner. The organisers do not design a progression from white to red or from village to grand cru; the wine comes from the guests' cellars. A single table may hold a Meursault premier cru, a Puligny-Montrachet, a Volnay, a Pommard or something much older, and it may also hold bottles unrelated to the theme. This is not a structured masterclass but a horizontal comparison among Burgundian growers and merchants. The Paulée literary prize places wine and writing in the same ceremony, a reminder that Burgundy's reputation is built not only from vineyard classifications but also from the language and sociability that grow around the wine.

Taking part on site

The Paulée is not freely sold to the public. Historically, seats have been allocated through the Paulée organisation and Meursault's town hall, and in recent years seats have also been obtained through organiser announcements and invitations; there is no single public ticketing page. Anyone planning to attend should follow the organiser's and local Meursault announcements from late summer to early autumn, confirm directly with the organiser, and avoid secondary resale or “guaranteed entry” travel packages. The organiser has not published a 2026 ticket price or menu, and this event does not invent one.

If you do get a seat, the rhythm differs from a restaurant. Guests arrive around midday for an aperitif and then sit down. Long tables mean sharing bottles with strangers, so asking a neighbour before pouring is basic manners. Bringing wine does not mean unlimited drinking: the event takes place in daylight, many guests still have to drive or catch a train, and spittoons and water are usually more useful than finishing every bottle. Do not bring large-format bottles as a display that conflicts with the wines provided by the house, and do not pressure others to drink.

For transport, Meursault is about eight kilometres south of Beaune, and the Paris–Lyon TGV stops at Beaune. From Beaune to Meursault, take a taxi or a pre-booked local car; public transport is limited. Traffic between Beaune and Meursault is concentrated on the day, so do not leave arrival until ten minutes before the start. If you stay in Beaune, book a return car in advance; if you stay in Meursault, you can walk to the château but should still allow time for check-in and entry.

Wine and the city

Meursault is one of the most important white-wine villages of the Côte de Beaune, known for Chardonnay. Château de Meursault's vineyard covers about 67 hectares across more than a hundred parcels in and around Meursault, including six grands crus and twenty premiers crus. The Clos du Château, about eight hectares at the foot of the estate, is one of the sources of its flagship white. The underground cellars were dug and used from the 12th to the 19th century and cover about 3,500 square metres; the estate completed a renovation in 2024, and the new winery saw its first harvest in 2026. The Paulée places a village banquet inside a working estate rather than a hotel ballroom, closing the circle from land to cellar to table.

For Meursault, the Paulée is the occasion that best explains how the village organises itself. It is not a mass-market ticketed event but something sustained by growers, merchants, restaurants, the municipality and long-term relationships. It is not a tourist performance either: many guests are Burgundy professionals. What an outside visitor sees is one cross-section of the day: long tables, bottles, a menu, a prize and conversation. Understanding that is what prevents the Paulée from being read as a Burgundy experience you can simply buy.

Disputes and limits

First, the Paulée is not an auction. The auction is the Hospices de Beaune sale in Beaune on Sunday; the Paulée is the Monday lunch in Meursault. Writing them as one event misleads readers about the date, place and way of taking part.

Second, the edition number needs caution. Local wine reporting calls 2026 the 93rd edition, but the organiser has not confirmed that word for word on a public page, and different sources may count editions differently. This event attributes the figure to local reporting rather than presenting it as official.

Third, do not confuse the two Paulées. 16 November is the Paulée de Meursault, part of the Trois Glorieuses; 17 October was the Paulée du Château de Meursault, the estate's own 22nd dinner. The first is a village and regional ritual, the second a client event, with completely different prices, menus and access rules.

Fourth, ticketing is opaque. The Paulée has no single public ticketing platform and has not published a 2026 price. Any resale claiming to “guarantee a seat” deserves suspicion. Prospective guests should contact the organiser or local Meursault institutions directly and rely on the official reply.

Fifth, the event has not yet taken place. event_status is upcoming; date, venue, menu and guests may change. The 2025 edition was held outside the walls and the 2026 edition returns to the estate's vat room, which shows that the venue is not permanently fixed. Check the organiser's notice again before travelling and do not rely on this event alone.

Photographs are credited in the figure captions; no map is drawn. Château de Meursault is a fixed building; its address and the route from Beaune are described on the place page. The entrance, parking and check-in position for the Paulée are arranged separately by the organiser, and a single diagram would be misleading, so no route map is drawn.
