Shaoxing Yellow Wine Trail: museum, Xianheng and the central cellar

A one-day route that splits Shaoxing yellow wine into three layers. The China Yellow Wine Museum explains history, craft and custom; Xianheng Hotel on Lu Xun Middle Road brings the wine into a meal; Guyuelongshan's central cellar shows jars, vintage and time. The three stops answer how it is made, how it is drunk and how it is stored.

Shaoxing yellow wine is not just a drink. It has a technical chain and a thick layer of city life: starter and wheat qu, winter brewing and mash turning, clay jars and vintage, and only then a bowl on a table. This one-day route uses three stops to show how it is made, how it is drunk and how it is stored. The stops are not in one walking circle, but taxis link them comfortably.

Route at a glance

  • Stop 1: the China Yellow Wine Museum, 557 Xia Da Road, Yuecheng District, beside Guangxiang Bridge; open 09:00–17:00; allow 1.5–2.5 hours.
  • Stop 2: Xianheng Hotel, 179 Lu Xun Middle Road; opened in 1894; order a small spread in the ground-floor dining hall; allow 1–1.5 hours.
  • Stop 3: Guyuelongshan central cellar, Qiantao Road, Yuecheng District, also described as Sunduan Subdistrict; the official tourism page gives 08:00–16:00; allow about an hour.
  • Movement: museum to Xianheng is about three kilometres on foot or by taxi; Xianheng to the cellar is on the ten-kilometre scale and needs a taxi or car.
  • Rhythm: museum at 09:00, Xianheng around 11:30, cellar at about 13:30, back in the old city by 15:30. The cellar closes at 16:00, so do not reverse the order.

Why these three stops

The museum provides the system: history, drinking customs, technique, Huadiao decoration and an underground cellar, placing Shaoxing wine inside rice farming, waterways, seasons and ritual. Xianheng provides the everyday: Lu Xun's "Kong Yiji" made "two bowls of wine and a plate of fennel beans" part of city life, and the real hotel still sells wine with small dishes. The central cellar provides time: the wine is still in the jars, and vintage is a position in the warehouse and a process of ageing.

Each stop has a boundary. The museum is an exhibition space, not a bar; Xianheng is a place to eat and drink, not a memorial hall; the cellar is a storage and industrial-tourism site, not a full production line. Keeping these layers apart stops you from looking for a wine glass in the museum or a winery lunch in the cellar.

Stop 1: China Yellow Wine Museum

The museum stands in the north of the old city, beside Guangxiang Bridge, at 557 Xia Da Road. Invested in by Zhejiang Guyuelongshan Shaoxing Wine Co., it opened in 2007 and covers about 30,000 square metres, with roughly 16,000 square metres of building. The route runs through a preface hall, wine-history hall, wine-customs hall, wine-industry hall, wine-technique hall, Huadiao hall, an underground cellar and a manual workshop. The ground floor leans toward history and ritual, the upper floor toward craft and decoration.

Take history and customs first, then craft, then go down to the cellar. The customs hall rewards slow reading: a daughter's birth is marked by burying "daughter's red", a successful examination by opening "top scholar's red", a wedding by sharing a cup, and Qingming, Dragon Boat, Mid-Autumn and Double Ninth each have their own wine and food. The technique hall separates steeping, steaming, spreading, vatting, turning, pressing, boiling and sealing; after that, the cellar makes sense as the result of temperature, humidity, container and vintage.

Ticket, guide and tasting policies change, and adult, senior and family tickets differ across platforms, so check notices before setting out. Minors must not drink. The cellar is cool and the floor may be damp; bring a light layer in summer and do not touch jars or displays.

Stop 2: Xianheng Hotel

From the museum, head southeast through the old city to 179 Lu Xun Middle Road. Xianheng opened in 1894; its name comes from the I Ching phrase "all things flourish", and it became famous through Lu Xun's "Kong Yiji". The present building results from a 1981 reconstruction and a post-2007 expansion, and the ground-floor dining hall keeps the late-Qing wine-house style: an L-shaped counter, the "Taibai's legacy" sign, jars by the door and blue-rimmed bowls. It is not Lu Xun's original building, but it is not an invented set either.

The first order is usually yellow wine. Shaoxing yellow wine is based on glutinous rice, wheat qu and Jian Lake water; common types include Yuanhong, Jiafan, Shanniang and Xiangxue, usually 14%–20% alcohol. Xianheng's Taidiao is a blended aged Jiafan, sweeter and fuller than ordinary Jiafan. Fennel beans are broad beans braised with star anise and cinnamon, not peanuts; stinky tofu, drunken chicken, soy duck and preserved-vegetable pork are also common.

Know which area you are in. The street-side dining hall is semi-self-service: order and collect at the counter, then find a seat. The restaurant and private rooms are full-service. For a first visit, order small and varied: fennel beans, stinky tofu, drunken chicken and a small bowl of warm wine. Yellow wine catches up with you; do not drink it at beer speed, and eat something first.

Read the cultural layer correctly: Kong Yiji is fictional, the hotel is real, and the "owes nineteen coppers" sign is a later materialisation of literary memory, not a historical ledger. At holidays the lunch queue is long; avoid 11:30–13:00 and 17:30–19:00 for a quieter meal.

Stop 3: Guyuelongshan central cellar

Take a taxi from Xianheng to Qiantao Road; expect to leave the old city. The cellar stands on Qiantao Road in Yuecheng District, also described as Sunduan Subdistrict. Shaoxing News reported in 2025 that the site covers 308 mu, has 28 large cellars and holds 260,000 kilolitres, about 1100 ten-thousand jars of base wine, roughly 11 million, making it one of China's largest yellow-wine cellars. A 2026 culture-and-tourism page gives 08:00–16:00 and notes that it was built in the mid-twentieth century with funds allocated on Premier Zhou Enlai's instruction.

What you see is not a production line but a place of ageing. Clay jars have slight permeability; in a stable warehouse the wine slowly oxidises and forms esters. Sharp alcohol becomes rounder, and aroma moves from rice and wheat qu toward aged, nutty and dried-fruit notes. Vintage tags, seals, jar markings and the division of the cellars are the physical evidence behind "aged for the full term". The site has added exhibition and sales functions, but storage remains the main job.

An hour is enough. Do not touch jar mouths, seals or vintage tags, and do not open cellar doors. The floor may be damp; wear shoes with grip. If a tasting is offered, ask whether it is included. Yellow wine is 14%–20% alcohol, so do not drive afterwards. Groups should contact the company in advance; independent visitors should confirm walk-in access.

Craft: wheat qu, starter and winter brewing

Shaoxing yellow wine ferments with wheat qu and wine starter. Wheat qu, made from wheat, supplies saccharifying enzymes and aroma and gives the wine its frame. Wine starter, made from rice flour and herbs such as smartweed and inoculated with moulds and yeasts, starts fermentation. Steamed glutinous rice is cooled, mixed with qu and starter, and put into vats, where saccharification and fermentation proceed together. The mash is turned, or "opened", to release heat and admit oxygen; the winemaker reads temperature, bubbles and smell rather than a clock.

Winter brewing is the key seasonal idea. Cold suppresses competing microbes and slows fermentation, giving a cleaner wine with finer aromas. Traditionally the weeks around the winter solstice are the key period for starting the mash; a long, cool fermentation is followed by pressing, clarification, heating and storage in clay jars. Heating stabilises the wine and prepares it for ageing. After this sequence, the jars make sense: vintage is the result of temperature, microbes and container. Jian Lake water is the other thread; the traditional production area sits around the lake, and its mineral and microbial profile influence fermentation and texture.

Transport, seasons and drinking boundaries

In the old city, walk and take short taxi rides. The museum is served by buses 5, 39, 51, 108 and 168 near Xiaocheng Beiqiao or International Mall; Xianheng is about six minutes on foot from Lu Xun Hometown station on metro line 1. The museum to Xianheng is about three kilometres: walk in good weather, take a taxi in summer or rain. Xianheng to the cellar needs a taxi or car; old-city lanes are narrow, so park in a public car park rather than at a hotel door.

Spring and autumn are the most comfortable seasons. Shaoxing is hot and humid in summer, so replace the museum–Xianheng walk with a taxi; winter is damp and cold, but winter brewing is underway, and anyone interested in the craft can watch for seasonal events, subject to official announcements. The cellar is indoors and works in rain, though the floor may be damp.

Set drinking boundaries in advance. The museum and cellar are for visiting, not for opening bottles; Xianheng is the one place to sit down with a glass. Yellow wine is 14%–20% alcohol; a small bowl is enough, and it should not be drunk at the speed of beer or baijiu. Do not drive after tasting; taxis and the metro are easy, and minors must not drink.
