Alsace Grand Cru trail: Kaysersberg and Riquewihr

From Domaine Weinbach in Kaysersberg to Hugel in Riquewihr, both stops sit in the rain shadow of the Vosges. The route links the 51 Grand Cru sites, the four noble grapes and the logic of Vendanges Tardives and Sélection de Grains Nobles; the drive is short, the structure broad.

Armagnac trail: single estates of Bas-Armagnac

From Château de Laubade in Sorbets, Gers, to Domaine d'Ognoas in Arthez-d'Armagnac, Landes, two single estates line up across the tawny sands of Bas-Armagnac. One uses a 1974 continuous still and three thousand casks for vintage work; the other grew from a medieval seigneurie into a departmental showcase. The route links continuous distillation, vintage labels and the Gascon countryside in a day and a half.

Barbados rum trail: Saint Lucy and Saint Philip

From Mount Gay in the north of Saint Lucy to Foursquare in the south-east of Saint Philip, about an hour apart. One stop explains continuity through a 1703 well and pot-plus-column distillation; the other explains transparency through an open-house sugar factory and a no-added-sugar position.

A walking route through Scotland's smallest protected whisky region, linking Springbank in Well Close with Glen Scotia on High Street. Less than a kilometre apart, the two represent a distillery that keeps the whole process on site and a Victorian plant that has changed hands repeatedly but kept its old scale, compressing Campbeltown's boom and retreat into half a day.

On a street of about a kilometre and a half, read Champagne through its houses and its hills at once. Walk the façades, courtyards and cellar entrances, descend into Moët's 28 kilometres of galleries, then continue to Perrier-Jouët for its Art Nouveau house, cellars and Chardonnay-led cuvées.

Cognac trail: the house belt between Cognac and Jarnac

A belt of houses along a dozen kilometres of the Charente, with both stops inside Cognac town: the Hennessy quay and the Rémy Martin historic house. One shows a large house running ten tours as a brand flagship; the other breaks a Fine Champagne promise into three visitor sites. The background is six crus, Ugni Blanc, double distillation and the VS/VSOP/XO scale.

From the brand museum at Avenida del Puerto 262 in old Havana to the local industry museum at Calle San Basilio 358 in Santiago de Cuba. The cities are more than 800 km apart and share one thread: aguardiente to ron, 1862, nationalisation and the export system.
