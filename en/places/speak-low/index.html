Setting and history

Speak Low is at 579 Fuxing Middle Road in Shanghai's former French Concession, near Ruijin Second Road. It opened in 2014 as Shingo Gokan's first bar in Shanghai. The name comes from the drink with which he won the global Bacardi Legacy competition in 2012—an original built on rum, sherry, yuzu and matcha. Turning a competition drink into a bar name, and placing it in a "what if Prohibition had happened in Shanghai" setting, was the bar's original story.

Gokan had worked at Angel's Share in New York and knew both the Japanese and the New York bar rhythms. Speak Low did not copy New York as an American sports bar; it layered the Prohibition-era hidden entrance, the street scale of the old French Concession and the Japanese attention to detail. In 2017 it ranked No. 10 on The World's 50 Best Bars list and No. 2 on Asia's 50 Best Bars; it has appeared on both lists many times since. Rankings are not everything, but they show that the bar shaped how Shanghai cocktail bars looked in the years after it opened.

Speak Low is not a century-old institution, nor a fully preserved historic building; it uses the old buildings and street atmosphere of the former French Concession, with an entrance designed to look like a bar-tool shop. Understanding this keeps the "hidden entrance" from reading as pure marketing: it also solves the practical problem of a small street frontage and the need to draw guests upstairs.

The space and the list

The entrance is inside OCHO, the bar-tool shop: push the concealed door and climb the stairs. The first flight carries you from the street-level retail space to the bar floor, and that "see the goods, then see the drinks" order is part of the Speak Low experience. Do not hurry past OCHO as an ordinary tool shop; if you cannot find the entrance, ask the staff.

The space is layered and the mood changes. The second floor is the main bar, with a copper-top counter, brighter light and a faster rhythm—good for a first visit and for the "New York" energy. The third floor is quieter, focused on Japanese whisky, selected spirits and classic cocktails—good for a slower drink. The 50 Best entry describes the building as multi-level, becoming more private as you go up; which floor is open, members-only or booked for a private event depends on the day and official channels.

The list is built on classics and original drinks. On a first visit, order the eponymous Speak Low to decide whether you like the acidity and tea note of rum, sherry, yuzu and matcha; or let the bartender recommend by preference. The second floor leans social, with fruit-led, refreshing long drinks; the third leans whisky and clearly structured classics. Drinks change by season, and prices follow the official menu (page verified 2026-11-08).

Prices are mid-to-high for Shanghai's high-end cocktail bars; budget for "two drinks and a snack per person" rather than for an ordinary neighbourhood bar. The room is small, and weekends and holidays often bring a wait; do not plan on the assumption that a hidden bar means no queue.

What to order on a first visit

Find OCHO at 579 Fuxing Middle Road and go in; do not stop at the shelves. The concealed door is inside the shop, and the stairs lead up. On a first visit, go to the second floor: the mood is more open and it is easier to read the bar's basic style; if you want quiet afterwards, ask whether the third floor is available.

Speak Low is best read by starting with the address, moving to the current opening status, and ending with what the place is known for.

At weekends, confirm the reservation method in advance; earlier on weekdays makes a walk-in easier. Smart casual is recommended—no formal wear needed, but avoid sports shorts and flip-flops. In a small room, do not talk loudly or block the stairs for photographs; if you want a picture of your own drink, ask the server first and keep other guests out of the frame.

Practical information

The address is 579 Fuxing Middle Road, Huangpu District, Shanghai 200041, China (Fuxing Zhong Lu 579), near Ruijin Second Road; telephone +86 21 6416 0133. Take Metro Line 1/10/12 to South Shaanxi Road, or Line 1/7 to Changshu Road, and walk about 10 minutes. The street frontage is the OCHO bar-tool shop and the entrance is inside it, so the house number is more reliable than a "viral bar" label.

Hours: Sunday to Thursday 18:00–01:30, Friday and Saturday 18:00–02:30 (Shanghai municipal government listing and Baidu Baike; the government page gives 18:30 on Fridays and Saturdays, a minor discrepancy). There is no admission fee and drinks are charged per glass; prices are at the high end for Shanghai cocktail bars, so budget for two drinks and a snack per person. Whether a service charge, minimum spend or booking fee applies should be confirmed when ordering. Parts of the second and third floors may be booked for private events or reserved for members; confirm by phone at weekends.

As of this check (2026-11-08) no credible closure or relocation notice was found: SG Group's official page, the Shanghai municipal government's July 2026 bar listing and Baidu Baike (tenth-anniversary renovation in 2024) all show the bar still trading at 579 Fuxing Middle Road. A "permanently closed" label on one aggregator has no authoritative support and is not used as evidence. No verifiable source for the coordinates was found, so latitude and longitude are left empty (unverified).

Etiquette and common mistakes

First, the hidden entrance is not an escape room; if you cannot find it, ask the OCHO staff rather than pushing shelves repeatedly or blocking the door. Second, do not treat this as a nightclub; the music level, seating and service rhythm are built for conversation and drinking, and loud noise spoils the room. Third, do not assume that no menu means no choice; you can say what you do not want and ask for a recommendation. Fourth, keep photography restrained; do not photograph other guests, and do not linger on the stairs or in the counter aisle. Fifth, tipping is not compulsory; if a service charge is on the bill, pay as billed.

Four myths are common: that a "hidden bar" means "no need to book" (weekends are the opposite); that every floor is open to every guest (some areas may be used for private events or members); that only the eponymous signature is worth drinking (classics and seasonal drinks matter too); and that Speak Low is "Shanghai's only cocktail bar" (it is an important one, not the whole scene). Another mistake is watching the ranking alone: a ranking reflects a jury in a given year, not your taste that night.

This bar does not suit large groups, people who only want to dance, people on a tight budget, or anyone in a hurry for a single standing drink. It suits people willing to spend time finding the entrance, sit down for two drinks and talk to the bartender.

Around and routes

Speak Low is on the stretch of Fuxing Middle Road near Ruijin Second Road, within walking distance of Fuxing Park, Sinan Road and Huaihai Middle Road; east lies Tianzifang and Xintiandi, west Hengshan Road and Xujiahui. Put Speak Low in the evening: eat nearby first, then walk to OCHO; or spend the afternoon in Fuxing Park and Sinan Road, have a first drink at dusk, and continue eating around Huaihai Middle Road.

Weekend evening traffic on Fuxing Middle Road and Huaihai Middle Road is heavy, and the Metro is more reliable than a taxi; Changshu Road and South Shaanxi Road stations are both within walking distance. To make Shanghai cocktail bars the theme, two in one night is enough; Speak Low works as the first stop because the space is layered, the list is readable and it is easy for newcomers. For more quiet, put the second stop in a smaller nearby bar.
