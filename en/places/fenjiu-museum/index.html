Fenjiu Museum

A specialist liquor-culture museum in Xinghuacun, Fenyang, Shanxi, with roots in a 1984 exhibition and a new building completed in 2007; nine galleries present Fenjiu's 6000-year narrative, historical drinking vessels and light-aroma production.

From Fenyang, drive 15 kilometres north to Xinghuacun, where the Fenjiu Group complex and the "shepherd boy points to Xinghuacun" sign sit by the road. The Fenjiu Museum is inside the complex, in a Ming-Qing-style timber building with red columns, painted beams and grey tiles, its plaque reading "Fenjiu Museum". It is a specialist liquor-culture museum and the core of the group's wine-tourism route: on one side a 6000-year brewing narrative, on the other historical drinking vessels, paintings and the production of light-aroma baijiu.

Setting and history

The museum's roots go back to a Fenjiu culture exhibition in 1984. It formally opened in October 1993, was expanded and reinstalled in 2004 and 2007, and the new building was completed in 2007. Official material says "the present Fenjiu Museum was established in 2007", while the National Cultural Heritage Administration annual report records October 1993 as the formal opening. The two are not contradictory: 1984 was the first exhibition, 1993 the opening as a museum, 2007 the completion of today's building. "Started in 1984, new building in 2007" is the safest formulation.

The museum presents itself as one of the earliest and richest specialist liquor museums in the Chinese baijiu industry. "Earliest" means within the industry, not "China's first museum". Its collection centres on historical drinking vessels, Fenjiu documents, paintings and literary works. The 2022 annual report records 1350 objects, a 12,000-square-metre building, 6000 square metres of exhibition space and nine display sections. Other sources give more than 4000 square metres, 8000 square metres or different gallery counts; these reflect expansion and different accounting, not fraud.

Fenjiu's historical narrative is long. The Xinghuacun site has produced late Neolithic small-mouthed pointed-bottom jars and other brewing, storage and drinking vessels; official and archaeological material therefore says brewing began in Xinghuacun 6000 years ago, and the group uses "6000 years" as its cultural spine. That number describes the brewing tradition, not the age of the museum; the museum displays objects, documents and techniques along that long line, not 6000 years compressed into one exhibit.

The modern key date is the 1915 Panama-Pacific International Exposition. Fenjiu won an award there, described in different sources as a "first-class gold medal" or "grand prize"; the museum holds a medal (the image on this entry) and reconstructs the award scene. Treat it as a symbol of Fenjiu entering the modern international market, not as the only proof of its history. More reliable is the brand-building, industrialisation and standard-setting that followed 1915.

The site and what to see

The building is Ming-Qing-style timber construction with red columns, painted beams and grey tiles, a broad front staircase and a formal, slightly theatrical presence. Inside, nine galleries are arranged as "A Thousand Years of Fine Brew", "Shanxi Merchants and Wine Rhyme", "Green Bamboo", "The Light-Aroma Banner", "Ink Affinities", "Wine Capital Vessels", "Marvellous Craft", "Songs of the Wine Sea" and "A Splendid Array". Different sources give nine, ten or nineteen units; these are different exhibition stages, so use the on-site guide.

Four kinds of exhibit matter. First, historical vessels: pottery, bronze, porcelain wine vessels, warmers and drinking cups from the Xia-Shang to the Republic, showing how vessels changed with ritual, food and craft. Second, Fenjiu history: the Xinghuacun site, Shanxi merchant wine trade, Qing distilleries and the modern factory, explaining the origin and technique of light-aroma baijiu. Third, paintings and literature: works by Qi Gong, Huang Zhou, Fan Zeng, Guan Shanyue and Wu Guanzhong, and poems and essays, showing Fenjiu's literary circulation; do not treat the gallery as a "museum of authentic masterpieces", because some works are copies or commissioned pieces, and labels should be read. Fourth, craft displays: starter-making, jar fermentation, separate steaming and boiling, distillation and storage, with objects, models and multimedia.

Outside the museum, the Xinghuacun Fenjiu cultural scenic area includes the Old Workshop Museum. The old workshop covers 9000 square metres and is a baijiu site museum restored in situ with uninterrupted production; it was listed as a Shanxi provincial cultural relic in 2004, a national key cultural relic in 2006, and a World Heritage tentative site in 2012. Many of Fenjiu's "firsts" — China's earliest joint-stock brewing enterprise and its first baijiu trademark — happened here. Visitors often confuse the Fenjiu Museum with the Old Workshop Museum: the first is a liquor-culture museum, the second a site and working workshop. Both are worth seeing, but one does not replace the other.

A first visit

Allow 2–3 hours. Start with the nine galleries, following "history—vessels—Shanxi merchants—craft—paintings—products". If time is short, prioritise "A Thousand Years of Fine Brew", "The Light-Aroma Banner" and "Marvellous Craft", which answer "where Fenjiu comes from", "what light-aroma is" and "how it is made". Then go to the Old Workshop Museum for the original site, pits, workshop and production; its living scene is more convincing than any panel.

The scenic area is large, and the museum, old workshop, Xinghua Garden courtyard and wine-culture square require walking or the shuttle. Wear flat shoes, carry water in summer and prepare for wind in winter. Photography is allowed in the museum, but no flash; the old workshop is a protected site and production area, so follow the signs before photographing.

The tasting usually offers small cups of Fenjiu, Zhuyeqing, Baiyu Fenjiu and Rose Fenjiu. The amounts are small; sip slowly. Drivers should not drink, or should only smell. There are wine shops in the scenic area; before buying, confirm price, vintage and packaging, and do not be swayed by "internal supply" or "raw liquor" talk. Check the label and production licence.

Practical information

Address: Xinghuacun, Fenyang, Lüliang, Shanxi 032205, China. Navigate to "Fenjiu Museum" or "Fenjiu Cultural Scenic Area"; about 15 kilometres from Fenyang, one hour from Taiyuan and half an hour from Pingyao.

Transport: By car, take the Qingyin Expressway and exit at Xinghuacun, or use National Highway 307. By public transport, take a bus from Fenyang bus station toward Xinghuacun and then a taxi into the scenic area; from Taiyuan Wusu Airport it is about 1.5 hours by car. There is parking, but it fills in peak season.

Opening hours: Summer 08:00–18:30, winter 08:00–18:00; holidays may alter this, so check the scenic area's notice. operating_status is verified, meaning Fenjiu Group's official site still showed it as open on 20 November 2026; this does not guarantee that it opens every day. Telephone 0358-7229217.

Tickets: The official page gives both "CNY 42 per person" and "CNY 50 in peak season, CNY 40 in low season"; the ticket office's price on the day is authoritative. Children under 6 or under 1.2 metres, disabled visitors, active-duty military personnel and visitors over 70 are exempt; children aged 6–18, full-time students and visitors over 60 pay half price with valid ID. The ticket usually covers the museum and scenic area; whether the old workshop is checked separately depends on the site.

Reservations: The National Cultural Heritage Administration annual report says no reservation is required, but groups, guides and the Xinghua Festival period should call ahead. Individual visitors can buy at the visitor centre. There is no strict dress code, but do not wear slippers or shorts into the production area, and follow fire rules in the wine stores.

Etiquette and common mistakes

First, do not treat "6000 years" as "the museum is 6000 years old". The 6000 years describe the Xinghuacun brewing tradition; the museum started in 1984 and moved into the new building in 2007. Second, do not treat the Fenjiu Museum and the Old Workshop Museum as the same place. The first is a specialist museum; the second is a national key cultural relic and production site. Third, do not treat the 1915 Panama award as the only proof of Fenjiu's history. The medal is part of modern brand history; earlier history lies in the site, vessels and documents.

Three more mistakes are common. One is assuming "light-aroma" means "light and tasteless". Light-aroma is centred on ethyl acetate, with clean, fresh, crisp character, produced by jar fermentation and separate steaming and boiling; it is not "watered-down baijiu". Another is assuming "raw liquor" is always better than the finished product. Raw liquor is high in alcohol and aggressive; finished Fenjiu is blended and rested for balance. The third is treating the factory as the scenic area. Fenjiu Group is a production unit; visitors follow the scenic route and do not enter workshops, warehouses or offices unaccompanied.

The museum is not right for someone with no interest in liquor culture who only wants a photograph, anyone bringing very young children into the production area, or someone who expects to see everything in one gallery. It suits anyone interested in light-aroma baijiu, drinking vessels, Shanxi merchants and Xinghuacun history, and anyone building a Shanxi wine trip.

Nearby and routes

The Fenjiu Museum is in Xinghuacun. North are the old workshop and the wine-culture square; south is Fenyang; east is the direction of the Xinghuacun site. Put the scenic area on a "Fenjiu Museum—old workshop—Xinghua Garden—wine-culture square" route: museum in the morning, Shanxi noodles at midday, old workshop and garden in the afternoon, back to Fenyang at dusk. About half a day with walking and the shuttle.

For a "Chinese baijiu" theme, add the Luzhou Laojiao cellars and the Shuijingfang Museum in Chengdu. They represent light-aroma, a strong-aroma living site and a strong-aroma archaeological site. A Shanxi route can also add Pingyao, the Qiao family compound and Jinci in Taiyuan, linking "Shanxi merchants—wine—historic architecture"; do not try three sites in one day, because Xinghuacun deserves a slow visit.

During the Xinghua Festival (usually March–April) and the sealing ceremony, crowds and traffic increase, so check notices and tickets in advance. The factory area is windy and cold in winter; wear a windproof coat. Open flames are prohibited in the wine stores and workshops; follow the signs.
