Xianheng Hotel

A historic hotel and restaurant at 179 Lu Xun Middle Road in Shaoxing, opened in 1894 (the twentieth year of the Guangxu reign) and made famous by Lu Xun's stories, especially "Kong Yiji"; the present complex was rebuilt in 1981 and expanded after 2007, and its old-style dining hall still serves yellow wine and fava beans.

At 179 Lu Xun Middle Road in Shaoxing, a white board with black characters reading "Xianheng Hotel" hangs under the eaves, with celadon jars and blue-rimmed bowls beneath. The counter is L-shaped, and at one end stands the "Taibai's Legacy" plaque. Many visitors come for the scene in Lu Xun's "Kong Yiji" — "warm two bowls of wine and bring a dish of fava beans" — but Xianheng is not a memorial hall sealed around a short story. It is also a hotel, restaurant, banquet venue and tourist landmark; knowing that prevents the disappointment of finding it unlike the textbook.

Who drank here

The story begins in 1894. In the twentieth year of the Guangxu reign, Lu Xun's uncle Zhou Zhongxiang and others opened a small hotel at Duchangfang in Shaoxing. The owner took the two characters "Xianheng" from the Commentary on the Kun Hexagram in the I Ching — "containing the vast and bright, all things prosper" — to wish the business good fortune and smooth success. The naming places a classical text, a commercial hope and street life on one plaque.

What made Xianheng famous was Lu Xun's fiction. In "Kong Yiji", "Storm" and "Tomorrow" he used the hotel as an important setting, and Kong Yiji's gesture of laying out nine coppers at the counter and asking for fava beans became one of the most famous tavern scenes in modern Chinese literature. The layers must be separated: Kong Yiji is fictional and the hotel in the story is a literary space; the real Xianheng existed, but today's buildings and furnishings have been rebuilt and expanded several times and are not the site of Lu Xun's childhood. The sign reading "On 6 March, Kong Yiji owed nineteen coppers" is a later materialisation of literary memory, not a historical ledger.

In 1981, the centenary of Lu Xun's birth, the old shop reopened with its late-Qing Shaoxing style intact, and the old-style dining hall was retained. From 2007, the second phase of the Lu Xun Hometown project, known as "Xianheng New World", began: apart from the dining hall, which was kept as a cultural landmark, the rest was demolished and rebuilt. The expanded hotel has about 57,000 square metres of floor area, more than 200 rooms and over 2000 restaurant seats, and presents itself as a high-standard cultural theme hotel built around Lu Xun, Jiangnan culture and Yue culture. Today's Xianheng is therefore "old name plus new building complex": the dining hall carries the literary imagination, while the hotel and restaurants handle modern hospitality.

The drinks list and the food

The first glass is usually yellow wine. Shaoxing yellow wine is made from glutinous rice, wheat qu and the water of Jian Lake; common types include Yuanhong, Jiafan, Shanniang and Xiangxue, usually at 14%–20% alcohol. Xianheng has its own Taidiao range, a blended aged wine in the Jiafan family that is sweeter and fuller than ordinary Jiafan and is one of the hotel's core products. In winter it is served warm, with the pot placed in hot water until gently heated; do not boil it.

Among the snacks, fava beans are the most famous. They are broad beans cooked with star anise and cinnamon, not peanuts; the "dish of fava beans" in "Kong Yiji" refers to this kind of drinking food. Stinky tofu, drunken chicken, soy-braised duck, preserved vegetable with braised pork, pickled offal and Shaoxing river fish are also common. The old dining hall suits "small portions, many varieties": fava beans, stinky tofu, drunken chicken and a bowl of warm yellow wine is closer to local eating than a large banquet; the formal restaurants suit family gatherings, with larger portions and higher prices.

Order according to the area. The street-side dining hall is semi-self-service: order and collect wine and food at the counter, then find a seat. The restaurants and private rooms offer table service. Yellow wine has more strength than its mild taste suggests; do not drink it at beer speed, and eat a few bites first on an empty stomach. Families with children can try yellow-wine ice lollies or milk tea, but minors must not drink alcohol. Menus, prices and hours are subject to on-site signs and official channels.

A visit route

Xianheng Hotel makes more sense as part of a walk through Lu Xun's hometown than as a standalone meal. A sensible order is: in the morning, the Lu Xun Hometown visitor centre and the Lu Xun Memorial Hall (usually free, with advance reservation), then Sanwei Study and Baicao Garden; at midday walk to 179 Lu Xun Middle Road for fava beans, stinky tofu and drunken chicken with a small bowl of Taidiao; in the afternoon visit Shen Garden, or walk north to the China Yellow Wine Museum for brewing and wine-custom background.

With only half a day, prioritise the Lu Xun Memorial Hall, Sanwei Study and the Xianheng dining hall. The memorial hall covers his life and writing, Sanwei Study shows his schoolroom, and Xianheng turns the tavern scene from the textbook into something physical. If yellow wine interests you more, put the China Yellow Wine Museum in the morning and return to Lu Xun's hometown in the afternoon; the two are close enough to walk or take a short taxi, though the old town is hot in summer and the day should not be overpacked.

The photographic stops are the entrance plaque, the L-shaped counter, the "Taibai's Legacy" plaque and the bronze statue of Kong Yiji. At busy times the queue is long, so do not block the ordering passage and do not rest a glass on the edge of the counter. The dining hall peaks at the start of lunch and dinner; for a quieter visit, avoid roughly 11:30–13:00 and 17:30–19:00. Lu Xun Middle Road is pleasant after dark, but the dining hall may already be closing, so check the hours first.

Tickets and transport

Xianheng Hotel itself charges no admission; meals, drinks and rooms are paid for as consumed. The old dining hall and the restaurants usually run separate lunch and dinner services. Online guides often give 10:30–14:00 and 16:30–20:30, but hours change with holidays and seasons, so check official channels. Reservations and rooms can be booked on 0575-85116666. The hotel sits inside the Lu Xun Hometown scenic area, next to the Lu Xun Memorial Hall; the surrounding Lu Xun Hometown, Shen Garden and China Yellow Wine Museum each have their own reservation or ticket rules.

The address is 179 Lu Xun Middle Road, Yuecheng District, Shaoxing, Zhejiang. From Shaoxing North high-speed railway station, a taxi to the old town takes about half an hour; Shaoxing station is closer. Metro Line 1 stops at Lu Xun Hometown station, about a six-minute walk away; local buses also serve the Lu Xun Hometown area. The old streets are narrow, so use a public car park. The hotel's site gives about 40 minutes by car from Hangzhou Xiaoshan International Airport, an hour from West Lake and 1.5 hours from Ningbo.

Keep tickets and prices separate: Xianheng Hotel has no admission ticket, but yellow wine, dishes, rooms and banquets all have prices; nearby venues such as the China Yellow Wine Museum charge admission or require free reservations. Etiquette and misconceptions

The first misconception is to treat Xianheng as a "Kong Yiji museum". Kong Yiji is fictional and the hotel is a working business; do not expect staff to perform the story. The second is to mistake today's buildings for the 1894 original: the dining hall preserves the old style, but the complex was rebuilt in 1981 and greatly expanded after 2007. The third is to assume "Xianheng" means one restaurant: the group has dozens of branches across China, and 179 Lu Xun Middle Road is the headquarters and flagship, not the same place as a Xianheng Hotel in another city.

In the dining hall, first work out whether you order in a queue or at a table by scanning a code; rules differ by area. Yellow wine can be warmed, but do not put the pot directly on an induction cooker; ask the staff. Fava beans are a drinking snack, not a staple; stinky tofu has a strong smell, so cautious companions may want a small piece first. Tipping is not expected, and the bill is settled at marked prices; if service is especially good, rounding up is a friendly gesture.

With older relatives and children, note that the dining hall floor can be wet and crowded; do not let children run between the counter and the wine jars. Do not use a flash in other people's faces, and ask in advance about commercial shoots and group activities. Finally, yellow wine may be weaker than baijiu, but 14%–20% is not low; do not drive after drinking. Taxis and the metro are convenient, so leave the car in the car park.

See also

Xianheng Hotel is a living entry point to yellow wine: the drink is not an exhibit but an everyday accompaniment to fava beans, stinky tofu and Shaoxing dishes. Its home city, Shaoxing, is a core yellow-wine region; placing the hotel within the rice-growing, water-town and ritual landscape of China makes it easier to understand why "warming a bowl of wine" is an urban habit.
